1. What is the Compound Multiplier?

The Compound Multiplier represents how many times your initial principal multiplies over a given period due to interest earning interest on itself.

For example, a multiplier of 4.66x means every $1,000 invested initially grows into $4,660, without making any additional contributions!

Multiplier = (1 + r / n) ^ (n * t)

Where r = annual rate, n = compounding frequency per year, and t = time in years.

2. The Complete Compound Interest Formula

When you combine a starting principal sum with monthly additions, the total accumulated wealth is calculated using two components: Principal Compounding and the Future Value of an Annuity.

A = P(1 + r/n)^(nt) + PMT × [((1 + r/n)^(nt) - 1) / (r/n)]

  • A: Final accumulated balance
  • P: Initial principal investment balance
  • PMT: Regular contribution per compounding interval
  • r: Annual nominal interest rate (decimal)
  • n: Number of times interest is compounded per year
  • t: Total time horizon in years

3. The Rule of 72

The Rule of 72 is a quick mental math shortcut to estimate how many years it takes for an investment to double at a given rate of return.

Years to Double ≈ 72 / Interest Rate

Example: At an 8% annual return, your money doubles approximately every 9 years (72 / 8 = 9).

4. Real Returns vs. Inflation

Inflation erodes purchasing power over long timeframes. To calculate your Real Purchasing Power, subtract expected inflation from nominal returns.

Real Rate ≈ Nominal Return - Inflation Rate

Example: An 8.5% stock market return with 2.5% inflation gives a 6.0% real growth rate.
